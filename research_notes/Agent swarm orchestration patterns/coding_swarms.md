# Practical autonomous coding swarms and orchestrator delegation

Research cutoff: November 9, 2026. Research only; recommendations below are inferences, not implementation decisions. Strongest sources are dated first-person operational reports and developer research writeups. Official documentation establishes documented capability, not demonstrated reliability. Undated live documentation and repository main branches are identified separately: their exact historical availability at the cutoff cannot be established from a live fetch. No explicitly future-dated evidence is used. Context7 was consulted for Claude Code, OpenCode, and Gas Town usage claims, followed by primary sources. OpenCode documentation returned materially different V1/V2 surfaces; do not combine their configuration syntax.

## How are tasks discovered, split, assigned, handed off, reviewed, and integrated?

### Takeaway

The most relevant pattern for Starfleet is hierarchical ownership with narrow workers and explicit upward handoffs, not a room of equal agents constantly talking. Durable tasks and completion evidence matter more than elaborate persona names; discovery and verification determine whether useful parallel work exists.

### Cited Findings

- **Cursor, January 14 and February 5, 2026 — first-party research experiments, not proof of a production-ready general swarm.** Cursor first tried equal peers coordinating through a shared task file. Agents held locks too long, forgot to release them, sometimes wrote without locking, and avoided hard work in favor of small safe changes. Twenty agents reportedly delivered only approximately one to three agents' throughput. Optimistic concurrency reduced overhead but did not produce ownership of difficult end-to-end goals. — [January report](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents); [expanded February account](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Cursor's evolving architecture is unusually relevant to autonomous delegation.** A planner → executor → workers → judge pipeline improved accountability but waited on the slowest worker and could not adapt quickly. A single continuous executor that planned, researched, spawned, monitored, reviewed, edited, merged, and judged became overloaded: it stopped spawning workers, did tasks itself, failed to merge outputs, slept, and prematurely declared completion. — [February account, “Adding structure and roles” and “Continuous executor”](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Cursor's final reported arrangement:** a root planner owns the goal but does not code; recursively spawned subplanners own narrower slices; workers execute narrow tasks in their own repository copies, without talking to other workers. Each worker returns one handoff to its requesting planner containing results, concerns, deviations, discoveries, and feedback. Receiving the handoff wakes planning back up, including after the planner thought it had finished. This is event-driven replanning, not a fixed plan followed by a global barrier. — [February account, “The final system design”](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Integration is a tradeoff, not a universally solved role.** Cursor removed a centralized integrator because hundreds of workers bottlenecked on it. It also deliberately relaxed per-commit correctness: requiring every commit to pass everything serialized work and caused many workers to pile onto the same unrelated breakage. The author explicitly suggests a separately green release branch. This is research throughput with temporarily broken states, not an endorsement of merging unverified code into a production release branch. — [February account, “Removing the integrator” and “Commit correctness”](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Anthropic, February 5, 2026 — concrete experiment with no orchestrator.** Nicholas Carlini's compiler harness created fresh Claude sessions repeatedly. Agents selected the “next most obvious” problem and claimed it through committed files under `current_tasks/`; Git races forced competing claimants to pick something else. Workers pulled, merged, pushed, and removed task locks. Failed approaches and remaining work were recorded in repository documents for subsequent sessions. There was no orchestrating agent or peer message bus. Do not cite this as validation of a hierarchical orchestrator or assume it used the documented Claude Code team product. — [original compiler report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler).
- **Task discovery can come from evaluators rather than a planner's imagination.** The compiler agents first worked on distinct failing tests, then different small open-source applications. At the Linux-kernel stage all agents hit the same first bug, fixed it, and overwrote one another. Human intervention redesigned the evaluator to mix GCC-compiled files with randomly selected agent-compiler files, partitioning the failure search. Parallelism resumed only after the problem became independently diagnosable. — [compiler report, “Enabling parallelism”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler).
- **Gas Town, January 13, 2026 — author's operational manual, early release.** The Mayor is a user-facing coordinator; polecats are task workers; convoys group related Beads work. The author advises fully specified work before dispatch and says Refineries are particularly useful for large, prespecified backlogs. He explicitly says not to swarm constantly: schema changes and other serial bottlenecks may justify only one or two workers. Sessions are handed off frequently, and regular cleanups remove stale tasks, processes, and workers. These are operating instructions, not measured reliability. — [emergency manual](https://yegge.ai/essays/gas-town-emergency-user-manual/).
- **Gas Town's documented lifecycle separates durable work from the agent process.** Repository documentation describes dispatch via `gt sling`, assignment on a persistent hook, worker submission via `gt done`, a pushed branch plus merge-request Bead, witness monitoring, and Refinery integration. The design document's March 7, 2026 audit marks dispatch, session cycling, orphan recovery, and cleanup as shipped. Current README describes a batched, bisecting merge queue with verification gates. However, current docs disagree on cleanup details (one says sandbox preserved, another says worker removed), so do not treat the exact destruction sequence as a stable contract. — [lifecycle design and audit](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown/blob/main/docs/design/polecat-lifecycle-patrol.md); [mail protocol](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown/blob/main/docs/design/mail-protocol.md); [live README](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown).
- **Independent test ownership has a concrete operational example.** Neil Macneale's April 16, 2026 DumboDB report describes separate implementation and parity-testing repositories with distinct write tokens, plus a coordinator with read access to both. All agents could inspect code and CI but could only request changes in the other team's repository through Beads. The author reviewed the initial test harness and task list before letting execution proceed. At peak there were 13 polecats. CI results generated follow-up testing tasks, and a separate testing worker verified a newly implemented feature before changing its expected-failure flag. — [“Two Weeks in Gas Town”](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-16-two-weeks-in-gastown/).
- **Claude Code documents two coordination topologies.** Subagents run separate contexts and report back to a caller; teams use independent teammates, shared tasks, and direct messaging. The live documentation describes pending/in-progress/completed tasks with dependencies, and no nested teams. Teams remain labeled experimental and disabled by default. These are documented product capabilities, not evidence of unattended end-to-end delivery. — [official teams documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams); [subagent documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents).
- **OpenCode provides delegation primitives, not a demonstrated full scheduler in the examined documentation.** Context7's V1 documentation describes description-based automatic subagent invocation and per-agent task permissions. The live agents page instead shows a V2 surface with different configuration and says General cannot launch further subagents. Treat recursive delegation depth, permissions, and lifecycle as adapter/version contracts rather than assuming every harness supports arbitrary recursion. — [official agents documentation](https://opencode.ai/docs/agents/). The mismatch itself is a documentation/version warning, not a claim that one syntax works across versions.

### Inferences

- Give every delegated slice a named owner, bounded scope, acceptance evidence, and a parent task. A parent must remain accountable for the result after spawning; spawning is not progress by itself. Cursor's ownership experiments are the strongest directly relevant evidence. — [basis](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- Make the handoff a first-class artifact: task ID, base and output commits, files changed, checks and results, deviations, discoveries, unresolved risks, and recommended next work. Wake the owning orchestrator on completion or failure, rather than relying only on polling or a worker's final prose. Cursor supports rich upward handoffs; Gas Town supports durable lifecycle events. The proposed exact schema is a Starfleet recommendation. — [Cursor basis](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases); [Gas Town basis](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown/blob/main/docs/design/mail-protocol.md).
- Separate planning ownership from low-level process supervision and privileged integration. Avoid giving one model all nine roles that overloaded Cursor's executor. A deterministic runtime can track liveness, budgets, attempts, and artifacts while an orchestrator decides what work is valuable. This separation is an inference, not a demonstrated Starfleet design. — [basis](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- Keep discovery open during execution: test failures, CI results, review findings, and worker discoveries should create linked follow-up tasks. For monolithic failures, improve the evaluator or serialize before adding workers. — [compiler basis](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler); [DumboDB basis](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-16-two-weeks-in-gastown/).
- Treat model approval and human approval as different states. The current Claude teams documentation explicitly describes cases where a teammate's plan is approved automatically or by the lead, not the human. Starfleet's user checkpoint must be enforced by its runtime, not inferred from a feature named “plan approval.” — [official plan behavior](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams).

### Gaps

- No source establishes the best depth/fanout, task size, or worker model for Starfleet's actual workload. Cursor's scale is not a controlled comparison against a smaller team.
- No comparable end-to-end completion rate, human review time, or accepted-change cost is published across these systems. Source lines, commit rates, and agent counts are not substitutes.
- The examined OpenCode material does not demonstrate a native, version-independent recursive delegation and isolated-worktree contract. Verify the exact installed version before design or implementation.

## How do shared workspaces, repository copies, and Git worktrees behave in practice?

### Takeaway

Separate model contexts do not imply separate filesystems. Strong coding examples isolate mutable workspaces while sharing durable task state and integration history; shared-directory teams are best limited to reads or deliberately nonoverlapping edits.

### Cited Findings

- **Claude Code teams do not automatically isolate teammates in worktrees.** Official guidance says to partition file ownership to avoid edit conflicts; its parallel-agents guide explicitly distinguishes separate-context orchestration from worktree isolation. Subagents can separately request worktree isolation. Because these are undated live docs, exact introduction dates are unverified. — [teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams); [parallel approach comparison](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agents); [subagents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents).
- **OpenCode user report, February 10, 2026.** Issue #12896 describes three concurrent tasks in one directory causing file and Git-state conflicts, corrupted staging risk, difficulty controlling individual workers, and UI clutter. It requests worktree-backed sessions and tabs. The fetched issue was closed by an inactivity bot on April 11, not closed as implemented. Evidence strength: concrete user report and feature request, not proof of a universally reproducible corruption bug. — [issue and dated discussion](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/12896).
- **Compiler experiment:** each worker had a Docker container, cloned a shared bare repository into its own `/workspace`, and pulled/merged/pushed against the shared upstream. “Shared codebase” meant version-control exchange, not multiple writers operating in one directory. Conflicts were frequent and agents resolved them. — [original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler).
- **Cursor experiment:** workers used their own repository copies. Common copies and artifacts imposed infrastructure costs: after limiting RAM, disk became the hotspot, with hundreds of agents compiling producing many GB/s of artifact I/O. Git and Cargo shared locks also surfaced. The author raises copy-on-write/deduplication as possible future optimizations, not shipped solutions. — [February report, “Infrastructure learnings”](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Gas Town:** its repository describes worktree-backed worker hooks and persistent worker identity with ephemeral sessions; witnesses handle stuck workers and recovery. A durable sandbox can survive the model session. Worktree state, session identity, task identity, and merged status are therefore distinct concerns. Evidence strength: documented architecture, with lifecycle audit dated March 7 but current README otherwise undated. — [README](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown); [lifecycle audit](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown/blob/main/docs/design/polecat-lifecycle-patrol.md).
- **Beads persistence changed materially during 2026.** Yegge's April 3 announcement says the earlier SQLite/JSONL bidirectional-sync architecture caused conflicts, competing sources of truth, races, and data loss, and announces Dolt-backed Beads 1.0.0 with embedded and server modes. This is the author's migration account and stability claim, not an independently measured concurrency benchmark. Old descriptions of a JSONL issue tracker should not silently be presented as the current storage architecture. — [version 1.0 announcement](https://yegge.ai/essays/gas-town-from-clown-show-to-v10/).

### Inferences

- Default concurrent coding workers to isolated worktrees or clones; keep shared read-only exploration cheaper. Worktrees solve filesystem/index contention but do not prevent semantic conflicts or guarantee secure sandboxing. Containers and scoped credentials are separate layers. — [basis: compiler containers plus clones](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler); [shared-directory failure report](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/12896).
- Bind the worker's repository location consistently across file tools, shell tools, instructions, permissions, and review artifacts. A prompt telling a worker to `cd` is not a complete isolation contract. This is a design recommendation motivated by the real shared-directory conflict report, not a claimed existing OpenCode capability. — [basis](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/12896).
- Model ownership and lifecycle separately: task claim/lease, model session, process/container, branch/worktree, produced commit, integrated commit, cleanup eligibility. Do not remove the only copy of work merely because the session is done. — [basis](https://github.com/gastownhall/gastown/blob/main/docs/design/polecat-lifecycle-patrol.md).
- Enforce task claims and expiry in ordinary software, not through prose reminders for models to release locks. Preserve an audit trail so duplicate work and abandoned claims can be recovered. — [Cursor lock failures](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents); [Beads migration experience](https://yegge.ai/essays/gas-town-from-clown-show-to-v10/).
- Make integration policy explicit: research branches may allow temporary breakage, whereas protected release branches should require verified gates and any required human approval. Cursor's integrator bottleneck is a reason to optimize a gate, not evidence that safety gates are unnecessary. — [Cursor tradeoff](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases); [Dolt's damaging automatic merge](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-01-15-a-day-in-gas-town/).

### Gaps

- No apples-to-apples benchmark compares clones, Git worktrees, containers, and shared directories for the same swarm workload.
- Live README/API details may have changed after a dated article. Pin commits/releases before relying on exact lifecycle and workspace behavior.
- Worktree isolation does not establish protection of shared ports, databases, credentials, external services, caches, or remote branches. The examined sources do not demonstrate a complete solution for those shared resources.

## What observed results, failures, and costs should constrain Starfleet?

### Takeaway

There is substantial evidence that swarms can build ambitious artifacts, but much weaker evidence of economic superiority or safe autonomous integration. The strongest stories expose expensive failures, human-designed evaluators, narrowing parallelism near completion, and ongoing supervision.

### Cited Findings

- **Anthropic compiler: ambitious result, expensive research artifact.** Across roughly two weeks, 16 parallel agents and nearly 2,000 sessions consumed approximately 2 billion input and 140 million output tokens, costing just under $20,000. The reported artifact had about 100,000 Rust lines and built Linux 6.9 across x86, ARM, and RISC-V. The report also lists reliance on GCC for the 16-bit boot path, incomplete/buggy assembler-linker work at demonstration time, inefficient output, and code quality below an expert Rust implementation. Human work on tests and the GCC oracle was critical. — [dated original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler).
- **Artifact warning should accompany compiler success claims.** The repository's human-written warning says code was not validated for correctness, docs may make false claims, and use is not recommended. Current README functionality may reflect later development; use the February report for claims about the original run. — [compiler repository](https://github.com/anthropics/claudes-c-compiler).
- **Gas Town early field trial, Tim Sehn, January 15, 2026:** four skipped Dolt Bats tests were assigned in parallel. The system created PRs and unexpectedly merged one despite failing integration tests; a colleague had to reset and force-push the repository. The Mayor initially said all bugs were fixed despite only two PRs; additional work existed locally but had not been pushed. Four PRs eventually appeared, but the author rejected all of them. The one-hour trial cost about $100, which the author estimated at around ten times his usual Claude Code spending per unit time. Strong evidence for that trial's failure; not an enduring failure rate for all later versions. — [“A Day in Gas Town”](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-01-15-a-day-in-gas-town/).
- **Later Gas Town field trial, same author, March 24, 2026:** the DoltLite development account reports all 87,000 SQLite acceptance tests passing after an intensive implementation phase and a total bill of approximately $3,000. The author says multi-agent parallelism was more valuable early; near completion he mostly worked directly with the Mayor. Beads remained useful throughout. This is a stronger outcome than January's failed trial, but is still a self-reported project, not a controlled benchmark. DoltHub also supplies the underlying Dolt technology, so the author has ecosystem interests. — [“A Week In Gas Town”](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-03-24-a-week-in-gas-town/).
- **DumboDB trial, Neil Macneale, April 16, 2026:** reports a working prototype after two weeks, 1,422 implemented parity tests with 47 still marked expected-failure, and 13 concurrent workers at peak. The author repeatedly inspected tests and actual database behavior. Token exhaustion sometimes killed workers while the Mayor waited indefinitely until the human prompted it to inspect them; interacting with managed tmux windows could also break operation, and agent messages could interrupt the author while typing. This is demonstrated supervised prototype delivery, not unattended production-grade database development. Security and permissions features were explicitly out of scope. — [“Two Weeks in Gas Town”](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-16-two-weeks-in-gastown/).
- **Yegge's April 3, 2026 release announcement acknowledges severe historical failures** including agents killing workers and repeated major data loss, and claims those issues were resolved before Gas Town and Beads 1.0.0. Evidence strength: primary creator acknowledgement for historical failures; “stable” and adoption anecdotes are creator claims, not independent validation. — [release announcement](https://yegge.ai/essays/gas-town-from-clown-show-to-v10/).
- **Cursor's throughput is not accepted-code throughput.** Its February account reports a peak around 1,000 commits/hour and 10 million tool calls over a week without intervention once running. The same account says the research browser was not intended for external use, correctness was deliberately relaxed, and occasional conflicts and broken states were accepted. No dollar total or controlled speedup over a competent smaller workflow is provided. — [original account](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Documented Claude Code teams cost more.** Current official cost documentation estimates roughly seven times the tokens of standard sessions when teammates run in plan mode. This is a scoped documentation estimate, not a universal multiplier for all swarms or an observed Starfleet cost. — [official costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs); [team architecture](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams).

### Inferences

- Starfleet should measure accepted deliverables per dollar and human review minute, not just agents spawned, commits, tokens, or lines. Start with bounded, independently testable tasks and compare one worker against small fanout before claiming swarm gains. — [basis: expensive compiler artifact](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler); [four rejected PRs](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-01-15-a-day-in-gas-town/).
- Budget and liveness enforcement belong outside the model: concurrency limits, spend caps, deadlines, stalled-task detection, failed-session recovery, and user-visible blocked status. Never interpret an orchestrator waiting as evidence that workers are healthy. — [basis: exhausted workers and waiting Mayor](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-16-two-weeks-in-gastown/).
- Distinguish completion states: worker reports done; commit exists; branch pushed; PR exists; required checks passed; review approved; integrated. The January Dolt report concretely demonstrates why a single boolean “done” is unsafe. — [basis](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-01-15-a-day-in-gas-town/).
- Expect diminishing parallelism as work converges. Reduce fanout when there is one shared failure or serial integration bottleneck; preserve the durable task ledger even in single-worker mode. — [compiler bottleneck](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler); [DoltLite late-stage experience](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-03-24-a-week-in-gas-town/); [creator guidance](https://yegge.ai/essays/gas-town-emergency-user-manual/).
- Keep independent verification technically independent, including write permissions where useful. Two agents with unrestricted access to change both implementation and its acceptance test are not equivalent to independent review. — [basis: split implementation/testing repositories and tokens](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-16-two-weeks-in-gastown/).

### Gaps

- Public reports are selected successes/failures, not representative samples. None proves a generally reliable hands-off software team.
- No verified current operating cost for Starfleet's models, prompts, repository, or evaluator exists. Do not extrapolate historical $100/hour or seven-times-token estimates as a price quote.
- No strong evidence found here that peer-to-peer chatter, many named specialist personas, or automatically increasing agent count independently improves accepted quality.
- This research deliberately prioritized dated original operational evidence over a catalog of projects. It does not establish a separate agent-farm outcome or performance claim, and does not endorse marketing agent-count numbers as validated scale.
