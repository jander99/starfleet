# Autonomous multi-agent reliability and empirical evidence

Research cutoff: November 9, 2026. Research only; no Starfleet implementation or configuration was inspected. Starfleet implications below are proposed design/evaluation choices, not descriptions of existing behavior. Primary reports and experiments are distinguished from recommendations. No external framework API/configuration instructions are supplied.

## 1. When do multiple agents outperform a single agent, and at what cost?

### Takeaway

The strongest evidence supports **selective parallelism on independently verifiable, decomposable tasks**, not unrestricted autonomous swarms. Research/retrieval gains must not be advertised as coding gains: a later controlled study includes small coding samples and finds degradation, while a successful large coding demonstration lacks a single-agent counterfactual. — [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system); [scaling paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296); [compiler experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)

### Cited Findings

**Anthropic Research: original production engineering report, June 13, 2025. Evidence strength: useful primary practitioner report; limited reproducible quantitative evidence.**

- Claude Opus 4 orchestrating Claude Sonnet 4 workers reportedly outperformed a single Claude Opus 4 agent by **90.2% on an internal research evaluation**. An illustrative task was identifying board members of Information Technology S&P 500 companies. This is not a 90.2-percentage-point increase, not a published success rate, and not a software-engineering benchmark. The article does not expose the underlying evaluation sample, absolute scores, statistical uncertainty, or a matched-cost ablation supporting this headline. — [Original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- Its separate BrowseComp analysis says token usage explained **80% of performance variance**; tokens, tool calls, and model choice together explained **95%**. This is an association within their analysis, not proof that multiple agents independently cause the gain. A better model could produce larger improvement than doubling an older model's token budget. — [Original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- The published consumption comparison is approximately **4× chat tokens for agents and 15× chat tokens for multi-agent systems**. The denominator is chat, **not a single autonomous agent**. Do not repeat this as “a swarm costs 15× a single agent.” Dividing 15 by 4 gives 3.75, but even that is an inference from coarse averages, not a controlled task-matched ratio. Dollar cost depends on model mix and pricing. — [Original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- Anthropic explicitly says most coding tasks have fewer genuinely parallelizable parts than research, and tasks requiring shared context or many inter-agent dependencies are poor fits. The report's main benefit is breadth-first exploration with separate context windows that compress findings for the lead agent. — [Original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- The authors started iterative evaluation with **about 20 real-use queries**, then used LLM judges to grade factual accuracy, citations, completeness, source quality, and tool efficiency, alongside human testing. Do not assume this initial 20-query set is the undisclosed sample behind the 90.2% result. — [Original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)

**Controlled architecture evaluation: “Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems,” arXiv:2512.08296; retrieved revised paper dated April 8, 2026. Evidence strength: comparatively strong controlled research, but narrow benchmark/task samples and model-specific findings.**

- The revised paper reports **260 configurations, six benchmarks, five architectures**, and three provider/model families (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic). Architectures are single-agent, independent workers with synthesis, centralized orchestrator-workers, decentralized, and hybrid. Authors state they standardize task prompts, available tools, and compute; results should be interpreted in that experimental budget regime, not as unconstrained maximum capability. — [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296)
- Four benchmarks—BrowseComp-Plus, Finance-Agent, PlanCraft, Workbench—each contribute 45 configurations (nine models × five architectures) and **50–100 task instances per configuration**. SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench each contribute 40 configurations (eight models × five architectures; Sonnet 3.7 excluded as deprecated), but use only **20-instance subsets per configuration** because Docker evaluation is expensive. **260 means configurations, not 260 independent full benchmark replications.** — [Paper, setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296)
- Financial reasoning: centralized coordination produces **+80.8% relative improvement**, with the project overview reporting mean success increasing **34.9% → 63.1%**. This is decomposable evidence gathering/financial reasoning, not code editing. — [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296); [MIT project overview](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/overview/)
- Sequential planning (PlanCraft): **every tested multi-agent architecture degrades performance**, approximately **−39% to −70% relative** to its single-agent baseline. Workbench effects are marginal, approximately **−11% to +6%**. The same broad “agent” label therefore hides large task-structure differences. — [Paper, Figure 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296)
- Coding-specific finding in the revised paper: **SWE-bench Verified degrades approximately −15% to −2% across multi-agent architectures**. Terminal-Bench is mixed: independent approximately **+2%**, centralized approximately **−19%**. These are small subsets, not definitive estimates for all coding workloads, but they directly contradict importing research gains into coding without testing. — [Paper, Figure 3 and setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296)
- The paper reports **17.2× trace-level error amplification for independent systems versus 4.4× for centralized coordination**. These are the paper's operational error metrics, not attack probabilities, whole-task failure-rate multipliers, or universal safety guarantees. Its independent architecture specifically uses **synthesis-only concatenation without analytical cross-validation or majority voting**, so it is not an ablation of every possible independent-review ensemble. — [Paper, architectural definitions and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296)
- **Version caveat:** Google's January 28, 2026 engineering summary describes an earlier **180-configuration/four-benchmark** study, +80.9% financial gain, predictive R²=0.513, and 87% architecture-selection accuracy. The revised paper has **260/six**, +80.8%, cross-validated **R²=0.373** (0.413 with task-grounded capability), retaining 87% on held-out configurations. Do not combine the older headline sample and fit with the newer coding findings. The 87% result is held-out configuration selection, not demonstrated universal task routing accuracy. — [January engineering summary](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/); [revised paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296)
- The revised paper and MIT overview are cautious about tool-overhead interactions under benchmark-clustered analysis; the overview explicitly says relevant tool-coordination interactions lose significance after accounting for benchmark clustering. Treat the widely repeated “more than 45% single-agent accuracy means never use agents” and “many tools always punish swarms” as observed patterns, **not portable thresholds or laws**. — [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296); [MIT overview](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/overview/)
- Additional limits from the live MIT overview: independent agents **share environment state on SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench**, despite not exchanging messages. Its error-amplification metric describes **additional computational work associated with coordination failures**, not a probability of wrong final answers. A separate ~45% threshold rule predicts the direction of gain in **94% of 16 validation model–benchmark configurations**; this is distinct from the 87% architecture-selection result. Absolute performance prediction was poor when an **entire benchmark was held out**. The overview is undated and may have later edits; use its clarifications cautiously rather than assigning them the January blog date. — [MIT overview](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/overview/)

**Large coding demonstration: Nicholas Carlini/Anthropic compiler experiment, February 5, 2026. Evidence strength: concrete first-party artifact/engineering experiment, not controlled superiority evidence.**

- **16 parallel Opus 4.6 agents**, nearly **2,000 sessions over two weeks**, approximately **2 billion input tokens + 140 million output tokens**, and **just under $20,000 API cost** produced a roughly **100,000-line Rust C compiler**. Author reports Linux 6.9 builds on x86/ARM/RISC-V and roughly 99% pass rates on most compiler suites, including GCC torture tests. — [Experiment and source-code link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)
- Development was clean-room without internet access; the generated compiler depends on Rust's standard library. However, x86 boot's 16-bit stage uses GCC, and the demo uses an external assembler/linker. Generated optimized code was less efficient than GCC with optimizations disabled; not all projects build, and new work repeatedly regressed old behavior. This is a substantial but bounded artifact, not a production-ready GCC replacement. — [Experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)
- The harness used separate containers/local clones, shared upstream Git, task claim files, frequent synchronization, and specialist roles. It **did not use an orchestrator agent** or enforced high-level goals. Thus it is evidence about parallel autonomous workers and harness design, not a direct benchmark of Starfleet's orchestrator/subagent pattern. No equal-cost single-agent run establishes speedup or quality benefit from 16 agents alone. — [Experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)

### Inferences

- **Starfleet recommendation:** retain a strong single-agent baseline and route to delegation only when task boundaries, deliverables, dependencies, and acceptance checks are explicit. Favor parallel investigation, independent test cases, disjoint modules with stable interfaces, and read-only review over simultaneous edits to one evolving hot spot.
- Evaluate two comparisons separately: **equal total budget** (economic efficiency) and **equal wall-clock deadline** (parallel throughput). Record total tokens including coordinator/messages/retries, dollars, latency, verified success, regression rate, and human interventions. Otherwise apparent swarm gains may simply buy more computation.
- The evidence warrants a small bounded team as an option; it does not establish a best agent count, provider mix, or delegation depth for Starfleet.

### Gaps

- No public sample-level reproduction of Anthropic's original 90.2% research gain was found in the original report.
- The compiler report provides neither a matched single-agent baseline nor a measured coordination-overhead breakdown.
- The controlled study's 20-instance coding subsets cannot establish organization-wide deployment reliability. Starfleet-specific repository/dependency shape, model versions, and review practices remain unmeasured.

## 2. What actually fails: coordination, context, verification, and shared workspaces?

### Takeaway

Empirical traces show repeated steps, lost context, missing information, premature termination, and weak/incorrect verification—not just hallucinations. Real coding experiments show even competent agents can converge on one blocker and overwrite each other; adding agents does not create task independence. — [MAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657); [compiler experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)

### Cited Findings

**“Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?” / MAST, retrieved v3 dated October 26, 2025 (initial arXiv record March 2025). Evidence strength: public cross-framework traces and taxonomy; observational, not a universal failure-rate estimate.**

- **1,642 traces from seven frameworks**, across coding, math, and general agent tasks. Taxonomy built through close analysis of **150 traces**, six experts, and **14 failure modes** in three groups: system design, inter-agent misalignment, and verification. Expert agreement **κ=0.88**; scaled few-shot o1 annotation agreement with humans **κ=0.77**, accuracy 0.94. Most of the large corpus is LLM-annotated, not manually inspected by experts. — [Paper, methodology and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657); [public repository](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST)
- The initial cross-system comparison uses **30 traces each** for ChatDev/ProgramDev, MetaGPT/ProgramDev, HyperAgent/SWE-bench Lite, AppWorld/Test-C, AG2/GSM-Plus, Magentic-One/GAIA, and OpenManus/ProgramDev: 210 traces. Expanded samples include 100-trace programming/model conditions, 206 OlympiadBench, 193 GSMPlus, 168 MMLU, and 165 GAIA traces. Models include GPT-4 variants, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B, and CodeLlama-7B. — [Paper, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657)
- Reported task failure rates range **41%–86.7%** in tested configurations. Because systems face different tasks/models and small samples, the authors explicitly warn that the cross-system figure illustrates failure profiles, **not a fair system leaderboard**. Do not phrase this as “all swarms fail 41–87% of the time.” — [Paper, introduction and Figure 4 caveat](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657)
- Observed modes include disobeying task/role specifications; repeated steps; context loss; ignorance of termination conditions; conversation resets; failure to clarify; derailment; withholding critical information; ignoring peer input; reasoning/action mismatch; premature termination; incomplete verification; and incorrect verification. The paper's chess example passed superficial compilation checks yet violated game behavior at runtime. — [Paper, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657)
- MAST-guided ChatDev interventions report **+9.4%** task success from role/workflow adjustment and **+15.6%** from high-level task-objective verification on ProgramDev. Treat these as author-reported local intervention gains; neither fixes all modes nor establishes a portable improvement percentage for Starfleet. — [Paper, Section 4 and intervention discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657)
- The older project website describes **200** manually studied traces and seven frameworks, while the revised paper describes the **150-trace taxonomy development / 1,642-trace full dataset** methodology. Use the paper's explicit version/methods rather than merging the two counts. — [Project page](https://sites.google.com/berkeley.edu/mast/home); [revised paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657)

**Practitioner failures and operating constraints.**

- Anthropic's Research prototypes spawned **50 workers for simple questions**, searched endlessly for nonexistent sources, and duplicated searches when scope was vague. Prompt guidance included specific objectives, output format, allowed tools/sources, boundaries, and effort scaling. These are concrete observations, not a measured optimal concurrency formula. — [June 2025 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- Its original synchronous worker batches could block the entire result on one slow worker; the lead could not steer running workers and workers could not coordinate. The article discusses asynchronous execution as a future improvement with harder consistency/error-propagation problems, **not as already deployed there**. — [June 2025 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- In the compiler experiment, independent failing tests enabled parallel work. Linux compilation initially became **one giant shared blocker**: every worker encountered the same bug, fixed it, and overwrote peers. The human author restored partitionability with a GCC oracle that compiled most files correctly while varying small generated-compiler subsets; delta debugging was still needed for interacting file failures. Frequent merge conflicts remained. — [February 2026 compiler report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)
- The same experiment reports agents spending excessive time reorienting in fresh environments and running tests, context pollution from logs, and regressions despite passing subsets. Controls included progress/README files, concise test summaries with full logs on disk, deterministic-per-worker randomized test subsets, and stronger CI enforcement. — [Compiler report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)

### Inferences

- **Starfleet recommendation:** distinguish task claims from file ownership. A task lock does not prevent another differently named task from modifying the same file or invalidating a shared interface. Prefer separate workspaces and controlled integration; serialize shared migrations, generated files, lockfiles, and central interfaces.
- Classify “no progress” precisely. Repetition resembles **livelock**; waiting forever on a child/barrier resembles **starvation or deadlock** depending on dependency cycles. The sources show repetition and blocking, but do not establish formal deadlock rates. Add acyclic dependency validation, deadlines, heartbeat/lease expiry, cancellation propagation, and repeated-action detection as engineering defenses—not empirically validated cures from these studies.
- Treat worker context as a deliberately lossy interface: return artifact locations, assumptions, provenance, unresolved conflicts, verification evidence, and remaining blockers. A fluent summary alone can hide missing context.
- Independent reviewer **roles are not statistically independent errors**. Shared models, prompts, data, tests, and the author's rationale can produce correlated mistakes. Have the reviewer derive checks from requirements and actual artifacts, not merely agree with the worker's summary; use executable/differential/holdout checks where feasible.

### Gaps

- No reliable general incidence measurements for recursive delegation explosions, formal deadlocks/livelocks, or workspace collisions were established. The 50-worker observation and overwrite incident are concrete examples, not base rates.
- No portable optimum for recursion depth/concurrency or quantitative assurance from merely adding another reviewer was found.
- MAST describes failures and some local interventions; observational labels cannot fully establish causal mechanisms, and LLM annotation introduces measurement uncertainty.

## 3. Which controls are grounded in practice, and what should Starfleet adopt cautiously?

### Takeaway

Production reports support explicit delegation contracts, resumable state, traceability, bounded effort, and external verification. Security experiments demonstrate that agent-to-agent communication can spread malicious instructions and combine privileges across roles; a worker boundary is neither a trust boundary nor an authorization boundary. — [Anthropic production report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system); [Prompt Infection](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07283)

### Cited Findings

**Concrete controls practitioners report using.**

- Anthropic Research persists the lead's plan outside the context window, stores completed-phase summaries externally, can restart fresh contexts, and has workers write artifacts to external storage rather than relaying every token. It reports deterministic retries, checkpoints, **resume-from-failure rather than restart-from-zero**, production traces, and gradual “rainbow” deployments that keep in-flight agents on compatible versions. These are deployed engineering claims, not comparative trials proving an exact reliability improvement. — [June 13, 2025 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- The same report emphasizes observing decision/interaction patterns while protecting conversation privacy. Traceability should not mean indiscriminately retaining credentials or sensitive prompt contents. — [Production tracing discussion](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system)
- Anthropic's December 19, 2024 “Building effective agents” recommends the simplest sufficient architecture, ground-truth tool/environment feedback, sandbox testing, maximum iteration limits, and human checkpoints or clarification when blocked. This is practitioner design guidance, not empirical proof of a particular approval workflow. The live article has been updated since publication; current tooling names should not be assumed to have appeared in the 2024 original. — [Engineering guide](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)
- The compiler demonstration used containers, separate local Git clones, task locks, logs, progress files, and CI, but also an **unbounded outer loop** and skipped interactive permissions inside containers. The author warns against running that on the real host and about deploying code never personally verified. This is a capability experiment, **not a least-privilege production template**. — [Compiler report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)

**Security evidence and an actual reproduced attack scenario.**

- **“Prompt Infection: LLM-to-LLM Prompt Injection within Multi-Agent Systems,” October 9, 2024:** a simulated application used GPT-4o and GPT-3.5 Turbo, global/full-history or local/partial-history communication, **120 user instructions across email/PDF/web tools** and **360 instruction/attack-phrase pairs** for scams/manipulation/malware scenarios. Web payloads were inserted into retrieved content by the experiment, not deployed on compromised websites. A separate synthetic CSV data-theft scenario required compromising at least three roles: document reader, CSV reader, and coder producing an exfiltration POST request. This demonstrates privilege composition through messages, not a measured production breach rate. — [Paper, experiment setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07283)
- Its separate social simulation uses **10–50 GPT-4o agents** in random pairwise dialogues with memory retrieval. It starts by directly overriding one agent's system instructions; that is a stronger initial-compromise assumption than an ordinary web injection. Reported propagation should not be generalized to all production topologies. Proposed LLM Tagging plus other safeguards mitigates tested attacks; the paper says tagging or conventional defenses alone are insufficient. Do not treat labels as a hard isolation mechanism. — [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07283)
- **“Agent Smith,” ICML 2024:** simulation of up to **one million LLaVA-1.5 agents** using randomized pairwise chats and an adversarial image placed in one agent's memory demonstrates infectious multimodal jailbreak. “One million” is simulated population, **not one million deployed production assistants compromised in an incident**. Practical defenses meeting the theoretical containment principle remain an open question in the paper. — [Primary proceedings page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/gu24e.html)
- **Invariant Labs, May 26, 2025:** a proof-of-concept using Claude Desktop and the official GitHub MCP integration placed malicious instructions in a public issue. A benign request to inspect issues induced reading private repository information and publishing it in a public pull request. The researchers describe real public/private test repositories and exfiltrated test-user details; this is a researcher demonstration, **not evidence of an identified unrelated victim breach**. They emphasize an agent-level architectural problem rather than a GitHub server-code bug. — [Original disclosure](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)
- The disclosure identifies **Claude 4 Opus** as the tested model and notes that Desktop normally asks users to confirm individual tool calls, but “Always Allow” policies remove active oversight. Researchers recommend granular repository access restrictions and monitoring; their own security-product promotion is not independent evidence of those products' completeness. — [Original disclosure](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)
- Simon Willison's June 16, 2025 synthesis frames the hazardous combination as **private data + untrusted content + external communication**. It is a useful threat-model heuristic, not a benchmark result. Multi-agent separation does not break the combination if messages can move sensitive data from a privileged reader to a network-capable writer. — [Practitioner analysis](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/); [multi-agent experimental demonstration](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07283)

### Inferences

The following are **recommended Starfleet controls**, supported by the failure mechanisms above but not all directly trialed in the cited studies:

1. **Supervisor-owned resource accounting:** shared run-level token/dollar/tool-call/wall-clock budget; per-task allowances; global active-worker cap; explicit maximum delegation depth and total child count. Charge all descendants/retries against the parent budget, atomically reserve capacity before spawn, and prevent children from silently resetting limits. No universal numeric cap is established by the sources.
2. **Explicit work contracts:** each delegated task names the objective, input/version, permitted tools/paths, dependencies, output artifact/schema, acceptance checks, deadline, stop/escalation conditions, and whether further delegation is prohibited. Default workers to no recursive delegation until evidence justifies it.
3. **Capability enforcement outside the model:** default read-only investigation, sandboxed writes, narrowly scoped credentials and egress, no implicit inheritance of orchestrator permissions. Delegation must not be a way to obtain authority the caller lacks. Evaluate the **whole reachable message/tool graph**, not each worker in isolation.
4. **Treat sources and peer messages as untrusted data:** retain provenance/trust labels through summaries, separate instructions from retrieved content, validate artifact references, and prohibit messages from redefining policy or granting permissions. Labels help models reason but cannot replace capability restrictions.
5. **Human approval gates:** obtain approval before starting implementation from a research/design phase, widening scope/cost/permissions, publishing externally, accessing new sensitive datasets, or irreversible actions. Bind an approval to concrete action/artifact/scope and invalidate it when those change. Require explicit reapproval at each user-designated boundary; one earlier “proceed” is not blanket future authorization.
6. **Isolation plus controlled integration:** separate workspace per writer, narrow ownership/claim leases, stale-lease recovery, verified base revision, integration queue, and integration tests. Separate workspaces avoid clobbering but do not eliminate semantic conflicts or incompatible shared interfaces.
7. **Verification with independent evidence:** reviewer sees requirements and produced diff/artifact, runs checks, records exact commands/results and artifact revision, and can reject completion. Preserve tests outside the author's unilateral control where practical; distinguish unit-test passes from task acceptance. Reviewer agreement is not a substitute for actual evidence.
8. **Durable run state and resumability:** stable run/task/parent IDs, dependency/status ledger, heartbeats, ownership leases, prompt/model/tool versions, artifact checksums or commit IDs, budgets, approvals, and compact handoff records. Checkpoint before consequential operations; make retried actions idempotent or reconcile their actual outcome before retrying. Resuming thought is easier than safely repeating an external side effect.
9. **Liveness controls:** acyclic waits, per-task timeout, bounded retries with backoff, cancellation propagation to descendants, no-progress/repeated-action circuit breakers, and explicit failed/blocked/cancelled terminal states. Avoid converting an individual worker failure into an indefinitely blocked parent.
10. **Audit without unnecessary exposure:** durable metadata/tool outcome logs, trace links for parent–child relationships, decision summaries, timestamps, integration history, and sanitized failure artifacts. Capture enough evidence to distinguish model errors, tool failures, budget exhaustion, security denials, and coordination failures; redact secrets and limit retention/access.

**Suggested approval-gated evaluation sequence (proposal, not implementation plan approval):**

- Gate A: approve representative Starfleet tasks and correctness/security criteria. Include sequential refactors, disjoint edits, shared-interface changes, research, adversarial retrieved content, crashed workers, slow workers, exhausted budgets, and resume-after-side-effect cases.
- Gate B: review a single-agent baseline against bounded orchestrator-workers at fixed budget and fixed deadline. Measure verified completion, cost, wall time, context-transfer loss, duplicate work, conflicting edits, recovery, and human interruptions; report uncertainty and failed attempts.
- Gate C: authorize wider autonomy only for task classes demonstrating benefit. Keep irreversible/remote actions independently gated; do not infer permission to implement from approval of a research recommendation.

### Gaps

- The reviewed sources do not establish a general safety proof for multi-agent prompting, reviewer diversity, or prompt-injection detectors. Measured defensive success on older models/attack sets is not assurance against new attackers.
- Durable execution and retry handling do not by themselves prove exactly-once side effects. Provider-specific API semantics/configuration were not researched here; implementation would require current official documentation and Context7 verification.
- This scoped review found no defensible universal concurrency/depth budget, deadlock probability, or validated Starfleet-specific permission policy. Proposed caps/gates require threat modeling and empirical validation.
